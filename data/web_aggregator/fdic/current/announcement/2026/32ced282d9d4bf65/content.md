I. SUMMARY   
Staff recommends that the FDIC Board of Directors (“FDIC Board”) approve the 
attached Joint Final Rule (“Final Rule”).  Through this Final Rule, the Federal Deposit Insurance 
Corporation (“FDIC”) would establish data standards for collections of information, as required 
by the Financial Data Transparency Act of 2022 (“FDTA”). 
II. BACKGROUND 
The FDTA was enacted on December 23, 2022 as Title LVIII of the James M. Inhofe 
National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023. 
The FDTA imposes requirements on the Department of the Treasury (“Treasury”); Board 
of Governors of the Federal Reserve System; Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, 
Treasury; Consumer Financial Protection Bureau; Securities and Exchange Commission; FDIC; 
Federal Housing Finance Agency; National Credit Union Administration Board; and Commodity 
Futures Trading Commission (“CFTC”)
1 (collectively, the “Agencies”), as described in more 
detail below. 
A. Statutory Mandate: Joint Rule 
The FDTA requires the Agencies to jointly issue a rule that establishes data standards for 
the collections of information reported to each Agency by financial entities under the jurisdiction

of the Agencies and the data collected from the Agencies on behalf of the Financial Stability 
Oversight Council (“FSOC”).2 
The FDTA requires the Agencies to consult with Federal departments and multi-agency 
initiatives responsible for data standards before issuing the Final Rule.3 
B. Agency-specific Rules and Impact of Jointly-established Standards 
The FDTA requires each Agency, other than Treasury and the CFTC, to issue individual 
Agency rules adopting the established data standards (each, an “Agency-specific rulemaking”).4 
A standard “established” in the Joint Final Rule creates a presumption that the FDIC will “adopt” 
such “established” standard in any Agency-specific rulemaking that the FDTA requires the FDIC 
to issue:  
• for existing information collections (as defined under the Paperwork Reduction 
Act of 1995 (“PRA”),5 described in more detail below) that are received by the 
FDIC from depository institutions under the FDI Act or by financial companies 
under Title II; 
• where the standards are applicable; and  
• when the standards are feasible; 
provided that, the FDIC has discretion to scale adopted standards to reduce any unjustified 
burden on smaller regulated entities and tailor adopted standards, as the FDIC sees fit in 
accordance with the FDTA. 
III. NOTICE OF PROPOSED RULEMAKING 
A. Overview 
On July 30, 2024, the FDIC Board approved a proposed rule (the “NPR”), with a 60-day 
comment period, that would establish joint data standards.

comments both in support of and in opposition to the NPR as well as comments that asked 
questions or suggested alternatives to the proposed joint standards.

IV. JOINT FINAL RULE 
Based on the comments received, the Agencies are proposing to issue the Final Rule 
generally as proposed, except that the Agencies are (1) not establishing the proposed joint 
standard of the FIGI for the identification of financial instruments (as discussed above), (2) 
specifying that International Organization for Standardization (“ISO”) 10962 – Securities and 
related financial instruments — Classification of financial instruments (the “CFI”) is to be used 
in the classification, rather than identification, of financial instruments that are not swaps or 
security-based swaps, (3) establishing ISO 8601 for dates without reference to the Basic format 
option, and (4) more explicitly stating in the joint standards that the Agencies may tailor the data 
standards they ultimately adopt or adopt data standards not established in the joint final rule. 
A. Collections of Information 
Pursuant to the FDTA, following the establishment of the joint standards in the Final 
Rule, each Agency is required to implement the standards, to the extent feasible, in their 
collections of information.    
The Agencies proposed to define the term “collections of information” in the FDTA by 
reference to the definition of that term in the PRA.   
Some commenters expressed concerns about the breadth of the definition of “collections 
of information.”  Other commenters requested clarity as to which collections of information 
would be subject to the proposed data standards, how those collections would be covered, and 
how the Agencies would treat ad hoc reporting.  
The PRA’s definition of “collections of information” is widely understood by the 
Agencies and public stakeholders, aligns with the purpose of the FDTA, and is consistent with a 
plain language understanding of the term.  To the extent questions remaining regarding specific 
collection of information, the statute provides the Agencies the authority to resolve those 
questions at the Agency implantation stage. As such, the Final Rule would define “collections of 
information” as proposed. 
B. Legal Entity Identifier  
 
The Dodd-Frank Act, as amended by the FDTA, requires the joint standards to include “a 
common nonproprietary legal entity identifier that is available under an open license for all 
entities required to report to” the Agencies.  The Agencies proposed to establish ISO 17442 –

Financial Services – the Legal Entity Identifier (“LEI”) as the legal entity identifier joint 
standard. 
Many commenters supported the establishment of the LEI as the legal entity identifier 
joint standard, noting, among other things, it would promote interoperability and improve 
identification of entities across jurisdictions.  Other commenters raised concerns that the LEI 
does not meet the requirements of the FDTA, that establishment of the LEI would impose costs 
and burdens on entities (particularly on small entities) who would be required to obtain LEIs, and 
that the LEI is not widely adopted and not fit for purpose for some entities. 
As LEI best satisfies all the FDTA’s requirements for legal entity identifiers (i.e., it is 
common, nonproprietary, and available under an open license),6 the Final Rule would establish 
the LEI as proposed. 
C. Other Common Identifiers 
The joint final rule also includes the identifiers discussed below.7  
1. UPI and CFI 
For swaps and securities-based swaps, the Agencies proposed to establish ISO 4914 – 
Financial services — Unique product identifier (“UPI”).8  For other types of financial 
instruments, the Agencies proposed to establish ISO 10962 – Securities and related financial 
instruments — Classification of financial instruments (“CFI”) code.9   
Commenters largely supported establishing both the UPI and CFI standards, highlighting 
that both standards are taxonomic systems for financial instruments that are useful for 
aggregating data and increasing global transparency.  Several commenters observed that the CFI 
is a classification, not identification, system and questioned how one would use CFI to identify a 
financial instrument.   
The Agencies proposed establishing CFI for the identification of financial instruments 
that are not swaps or security-based swaps.  However, CFI does not provide the means to 
“identify” any discrete, specific security, rather it enables classification of equities as a category 
(e.g., the category identifier “E” for equity securities and “P” for the subcategory of preferred 
shares).  As such, the Final Rule clarifies this distinction.  
As CFI and UPI together provide a taxonomic classification system for financial 
instruments, which best allows for mapping a wide variety of financial instruments across 
different markets, the Final Rule would establish the UPI as proposed. In response to the

2. Date 
For date fields, the Agencies proposed establishing the date as defined by ISO 860110 
using the Basic format option.   
Most commenters supported the proposed standard, however commenters also raised 
concerns with the use of the Basic format option.  For example, one commenter questioned the 
need for specifying use of the Basic format option. Other commenters opposed the proposed 
standard for a variety of reasons, including by expressing concerns about costs associated with 
revising existing systems.  
The Agencies agree that there is no need to specify the Basic format option because any 
user of the information can convert dates between Basic and Extended formats in a relatively 
straightforward manner, given the only difference between the two is the existence of separators 
(For example, “YYYY-MM-DD” as opposed to “YYYYMMDD” to display a date).  Given that 
this standard uniformly displays the order of the date components themselves and that it is 
relatively straightforward to convert dates between Basic and Extended formats, the Final Rule 
would establish the ISO 8601 standard for date formats but would not specify that the Basic 
format option would be required. 
3. State 
For identification of a state, possession, military “state” of the United States of America 
or a geographic directional, the Agencies proposed to establish the U.S. Postal Service 
Abbreviations, as published in Appendix B of Publication 28 “Postal Addressing Standards, 
Mailing Standards of the United States Postal Service. 
Most of the comments relating to this proposed standard were supportive. However, one 
commenter noted that long names, and not abbreviations, are widely used throughout the 
derivatives market and have been incorporated into industry standards. 
As compared to alternative numeric State codes, this standard is more widely used and is 
more conducive to use by both humans and machines.  Accordingly, the Final Rule would 
establish this standard as proposed. 
4. Countries  
For identification of countries, the Agencies propose to establish the country codes with 
the code(s) for subdivisions, as appropriate, as defined by the most recent version of Geopolitical 
Entities, Names, and Codes (“GENC”).

D. Data Transmission and Schema and Taxonomy Format Standards 
In the NPR, the Agencies proposed to establish four properties for data transmission and 
schema and taxonomy formats, derived from the requirements listed in the FDTA.12  
Specifically, the Agencies proposed that the schema and taxonomy formats will, to the extent 
practicable: 
• render data fully searchable and machine-readable; 
• enable high quality data through schemas, with accompanying metadata 
documented in machine-readable taxonomy or ontology models, which clearly 
define the semantic meaning of the data, as defined by the underlying regulatory 
information collection requirements, as appropriate;
