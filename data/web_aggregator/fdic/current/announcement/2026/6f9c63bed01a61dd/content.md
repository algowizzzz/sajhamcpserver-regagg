I. SUMMARY 
Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) staff presents to the Board of Directors 
(Board) for approval the attached Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (proposal or proposed rule) 
and requests authorization of its publication in the Federal Register with a 60-day comment 
period. The Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (FRB) recently adopted a 
proposal to amend Regulation O (FRB NPR),
1 its regulation implementing sections 22(g) and 
22(h) of the Federal Reserve Act (FRA),2 which govern extensions of credit by banks to their 
executive officers, directors, principal shareholders, and their related interests (insiders). The 
FRB’s proposal would, among other things, adjust certain dollar-based regulatory thresholds to 
account for economic growth and inflation. Such thresholds have not been revised in several 
decades and have become effectively more restrictive over time. Because the FDIC has 
separately adopted these dollar-based thresholds in its own regulation,3 revisions are necessary to 
avoid disparate treatment between FDIC-supervised institutions and other insured depository

IV. BACKGROUND  
Statutory Framework 
Sections 22(g) and 22(h) of the FRA impose restrictions on extensions of credit to 
insiders of a bank that is a member of the Federal Reserve System (member bank).5 Section 
18(j)(2) of the Federal Deposit Insurance Act provides that sections 22(g) and (h) shall apply to 
every insured bank that is not a member of the Federal Reserve System (nonmember insured 
bank) in the same manner and to the same extent as if the nonmember insured bank were a 
member bank.

become indebted to that member bank, except by means of an extension of credit which the bank 
is authorized to make under that section.

FDIC Regulatory Framework 
The FDIC has implemented sections 22(g) and (h) in section 337.3 of the FDIC Rules 
and Regulations13 in a manner generally consistent with the Federal Reserve Board’s Regulation 
O. For FDIC-supervised institutions, an extension of credit to an insider14 requires prior approval 
by a majority of disinterested members of the board of directors if the amount, when aggregated 
with the institution’s other extensions of credit to that insider, exceeds either (1) the greater of 
$25,000 or 5 percent of the FDIC-supervised institution’s unimpaired capital and unimpaired 
surplus, or (2) $500,000.

V. DISCUSSION  
The Proposed Rule 
The proposal would update the $100,000 and $500,000 thresholds outlined above using 
seasonally adjusted U.S. nominal GDP, as calculated quarterly by the U.S. Bureau of Economic 
Analysis, to align with the FRB NPR. The proposal would also simplify the manner in which 
both thresholds are calculated, by removing the dollar-based “floor” for each provision, leaving 
only a sliding scale (of 2.5% or 5%, as applicable, of unimpaired capital and unimpaired surplus) 
and a “ceiling” (of $400,000 or $2,000,000), which will simplify the FDIC’s regulation to make 
compliance more straightforward for FDIC-supervised institutions. Finally, the proposal would 
provide for future automatic updates to the thresholds every five years, also utilizing nominal

In summary, under the proposed rule, extensions of credit to insiders would require prior 
approval by an institution’s board of directors if the aggregate extension of credit exceeds the 
lower of 5 percent of the FDIC-supervised institution’s unimpaired capital and unimpaired 
surplus or $2,000,000. Extensions of credit to executive officers for purposes not permitted under 
the statute would be authorized only if the aggregate extension of credit to an executive officer 
does not exceed the lower of 2.5 percent of the FDIC-supervised institution’s unimpaired capital 
and unimpaired surplus or $400,000. Such thresholds would be subject to an indexing 
methodology and adjusted automatically every five years to reflect the cumulative change in 
nominal GDP (a measure of economic growth and inflation) during that period.
