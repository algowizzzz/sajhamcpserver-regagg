Domestic Stability Buffer - Decision Summary Note - June 2024

Decision

Today, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) announced that the Domestic Stability Buffer (DSB) will remain at 3.5% of total risk-weighted assets, a level announced in June 2023 and reinforced in December 2023. Accordingly, OSFI continues to expect all domestic systemically important banks to target a Common Equity Tier 1 ratio of at least 11.5% of risk-weighted assets.

Rationale

OSFI’s decision to maintain the DSB level reflects its current assessment that systemic vulnerabilities remain elevated and broadly stable, while near-term risks to domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs) are still low but rising.

Household indebtedness remains a concern although with no material shifts since the last DSB announcement. Uncertainty persists regarding the impact of future payment shocks on household debt serviceability as many mortgages are expected to renew at higher interest rates. Commercial real estate market vulnerabilities remain elevated across office, construction and development segments in the context of falling demand and cost pressures. Geopolitical risks have increased due to escalating tensions in the Middle East, which could impact commodities markets and global growth, potentially affecting the Canadian financial system.

Concurrently, near-term risks have risen somewhat but are still assessed as low. D-SIBs have continued to report solid performance and remain adequately capitalized, notwithstanding some further build-up of loan loss provisions and the return of gross impaired loan ratios to pre-pandemic levels. Business insolvencies rose earlier in the year but have since moderated, while consumer insolvencies have increased but remain near pre-pandemic levels. Financial market conditions have improved amid underlying signs of resilience in the economy and expectations of a soft landing.

OSFI remains committed to proactively adjusting the buffer as the risk environment changes and stands ready to act in line with our early response principle, as warranted.  At the same time, D-SIBs are expected to remain vigilant and prudent in their capital management practices, consistent with the elevated uncertainty in the outlook.
