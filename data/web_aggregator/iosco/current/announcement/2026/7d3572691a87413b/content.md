About the Public Interest Oversight Board  
The PIOB is the independent oversight body that seeks to improve the quality and public interest 
focus of the international audit, assurance, ethics, and independence standards developed by the 
international standard-setting boards: the International Auditing and Assurance Standards Board 
(“IAASB”) and the International Ethics Standards Board for Accountants ( “IESBA”), collectively, 
the international Standard Setting Boards (SSBs). 
The PIOB provides oversight of the standard-setting process to ensure that the standards are 
responsive to the public interest, including that they are developed in accordance with the 
principles of the Public Interest Framework. The PIOB also has the responsibility to appoint 
members to the SSBs. 
To learn more about the PIOB, visit the PIOB website.  
To learn more about the IAASB, visit the IAASB website. 
To learn more about the IESBA, visit the IESBA website.

Executive Summary 
The Call for Applications: Board Member of the Public Interest Oversight Board ( “PIOB”) is an 
official notice for the general public . It outlines the requirements and job description of a PIOB 
Member and invites highly qualified individuals to apply for up to five (5) vacancies on the PIOB 
Board, for an initial term of up to three (3) years, commencing on or before January 1, 2027.   
The PIOB is an independent oversight body that provides oversight to the standard- setting 
process to ensure that international audit, assurance, ethics , and independence standards are 
responsive to the public interest . The PIOB also manages appointments to the SSBs and the 
Stakeholder Advisory Council (“SAC”) to ensure a multi-stakeholder composition. 
The PIOB is a non-profit foundation established in Spain, consisting of a Board of Trustees and a 
Technical Committee, the latter being tasked with oversight of, and nominations to, the SSBs and 
other governance responsibilities relating to the International Foundation for Ethics and Audit 
(IFEA) which houses the IAASB and IESBA .  The Technical Committee has up to ten ( 10) 
objective and diverse members, known as PIOB Members, including the Chair, committed to the 
public interest. All PIOB Members are members of the Technical Committee, while only certain 
PIOB members are selected to serve on the Board of Trustees having additional responsibilities 
and obligations1.  
PIOB Members are drawn from a diverse variety of stakeholder s, with experience in the 
preparation, audit, oversight, delivery and use of financial and non-financial information, and with 
the ability to assess whether  the work of the  SSBs is responsive to the public interest and 
undertaken in accordance with the Public Interest Framework  and to assist in ensuring that the 
standard-setting system has appropriate financial resources to carry out its standard- setting 
responsibilities in a timely, and high-quality manner. 
A PIOB Member is a non-executive, compensated position, with expected total time commitment 
(excluding travel) of approximately 300- 800 hours per year . The PIOB typically holds four 
meetings per year, with typically three meetings in-person in Madrid and one held virtually. Also, 
members are allocated oversight responsibilities of the SSBs, which partly comprise of in-person 
observations of meetings of these two standard -setting boards and of the SSBs Stakeholder 
Advisory Council (SAC).

Monitoring Group  
The MG, is a group of international financial institutions and regulatory bodies committed to 
advancing the public interest in areas related to international audit standard setting and audit 
quality. According to its charter, the MG has the responsibility to monitor the execution by the 
PIOB of its mandate and to make recommendations to the PIOB.  
In a current and combined effort, the PIOB and IFEA are actively considering options to simplify 
and improve the international standard-setting system, including a goal of attracting more stable 
and diverse long-term funding sources. The PIOB and IFEA will present the MG with options for 
consideration and recommendation for action, focused on short -term improvements within the 
PIOB’s mandate. However, longer -term recommendations will be considered which may 
eventually result in reforms to the functions of the PIOB.  
The MG published its most recent recommendations, Strengthening the International Audit and 
Ethics Standard-Setting System, in July 2020. The objectives of the 2020 MG Recommendations 
are the implementation of a more independent standard- setting system that encourages 
participation from all stakeholders in the standard-setting process so that it is more responsive to 
the public interest and an accelerated pace of change in the development of timely, high- quality 
audit, assurance, ethics, and independence standards. 
Under the recommendations, the PIOB continues  to provide independent oversight of the 
standard-setting process to ensure that international audit, assurance, ethics, and independence 
standards are responsive to the public interest and are developed in accordance with the Public 
Interest Framework. The PIOB oversight is undertaken  throughout the standard- setting 
development cycle and evaluates how  the SSBs considered and balanced input from 
stakeholders. 
The PIOB’s governance responsibilities include the nomination and appointment process for the 
SSBs’ board members, protecting the SSBs from undue influence, such as commercial, political, 
and economic influence, in the public interest, and overseeing the ethics and conduct criteria for 
the PIOB, the SSBs members and staff.

ROLE SPECIFICATION  
PIOB 
Under the leadership of the PIOB Chair, the PIOB Technical Committee is responsible for making 
appointments to and providing independent oversight of the SSBs. Under its current mandate, the 
PIOB’s responsibilities include: 
• Oversight of the work of the SSBs  to ensure that standards are responsive to the public 
interest, including that they are developed in accordance with the Public Interest 
Framework and that standard-setting activities follow appropriate due process throughout 
the standard-setting development cycle; 
• Assessing the effectiveness of the SSBs and their respective chairs; 
• The nomination and appointment process for board members to the SSBs and members 
of the SAC;  
• Assessing the SSBs’ delivery against their strategy and work plan; 
• Supporting a multi-stakeholder funding of the standard-setting system; and 
• Protecting the SSBs from undue influence, such as commercial, political, and economic 
influence. 
The PIOB is accountable to the MG , which monitors the effectiveness of the PIOB in achieving 
its mandate in the public interest.

PIOB Members contribute primarily through active participation in meetings of the PIOB , 
observation of the SSB meetings, SAC meetings, outreach activities, and meetings with the MG. 
Therefore, it is important that candidates carefully consider whether they are able to allocate the 
time needed for meaningful contribution. The members of the PIOB will regularly engage with the

responsiveness. PIOB Members likewise will engage with stakeholders, particularly in their 
region, to assist in the identification of public interest issues , encouraging them to actively 
participate in the standards development process by making their views known to the SSBs , 
fundraising activities in support of the standard-setting system, and other outreach.

STIPEND 
PIOB Members are entitled to receive fixed annual stipends of EUR 20,625, and variable 
remuneration of EUR 825 per day of attendance at assigned public interest meetings. Stipend 
and variable remuneration information are subject to change in the future, and further information 
will be provided to PIOB Board Members upon appointment. 
Reasonable travel expenses incurred in performing duties on behalf of the PIOB will be 
reimbursed. 
TERM LIMIT 
A PIOB Member is typically appointed for an initial term of up to three (3) years.  
High-performing PIOB Members are eligible to be considered for  re-appointment for a second

whether any new candidates may be more suitable in the context of the PIOB’s priorities and 
composition when evaluated against the skills matrix.

Candidates should submit their applications to the MG secretariat at piobmgnominations@iosco.org. To be

Applications should consist of a CV and cover letter (two pages maximum), which should include the
