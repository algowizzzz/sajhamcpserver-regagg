Public 
 Summary of changes 
A number of common points were raised in the consultation process. The detailed edits on these points are reflected in section 2. This section 
considers the common matters raised and broadly explains how they have been addressed in the Application Paper:  
Issues raised Reponse 
1.1 Tone 
The paper focuses excessively on the risks of AI 
systems and not sufficiently on the opportunities 
the technology offers. 
The paper was perceived as creating overly 
burdensome requirements, raising compliance 
costs disproportionately, which could ultimately 
hinder the adoption of AI systems in the sector. 
The paper  
• Highlights AI opportunities, including a dedicated box in the introduction prominently.  
• Acknowledges both opportunities and challenges for financial inclusion enabled by 
granular risk-based pricing practices (see points 1.5). 
• Further emphasises risk-based and proportionality considerations (see points 1.3).  
 
1.2 Definition of AI systems 
The OECD definition of AI systems is too broad 
and could capture longstanding mathematical 
models used in insurance. Suggested a 
narrower, insurance-specific definition. 
Retained the OECD definition but made edits to: 
• Highlight the focus on AI systems with autonomous and adaptability features, 
excluding traditional mathematical models. 
• Encourage insurers to define models needing more attention. 
• Emphasise a proportionate and risk-based approach, scoping out low-risk activities. 
1.3 Proportionate and risk-based approach 
The paper is burdensome and introduces new 
requirements, raising compliance costs. 
Clarified that: 
• The paper does not introduce new standards; it provides guidance, not checklists.  
• Focus is on integrating guidance into existing risk and governance frameworks.  
• Emphasises a risk-based approach to supervision with a new section 2.

Public 
• Combines governance and risk management measures for responsible AI 
governance, based on the proportionality principle. 
1.4 Third-party oversight 
Insurers are expected to have control or oversight 
over third parties. 
• Clarified that insurers must assess whether acquiring or using third-party (and n-th) 
AI systems constitutes the outsourcing of critical services and require  that such 
arrangements meet the oversight expectations outlined in ICP 8.8 for outsourcing 
critical services. 
• Consistent with existing requirements, insurers should obtain adequate information 
and reassurances from third- party (and nth-party) providers, respecting intellectual 
property rights (eg via contractual clauses). 
• Recognises increasing importance of third-party risk governance and management 
with new AI models like GenAI. 
• IAIS will monitor developments through the FinTech Forum and Operational 
Resilience Working Group. 
1.5 Societal impacts of granular risk pricing 
More granular risk pricing enabled by AI may 
negatively impact protection gaps 
The paper  
• Highlights potential negative impacts on financial inclusion for high-risk customers, 
especially vulnerable consumers. 
• Acknowledges that some customer groups may benefit from greater access to 
affordable insurance due to granular risk assessments enabled by AI systems. 
1.6 Additional changes 
Requests for additional information to be added 
to the paper. 
• We received a number of thoughtful suggestions for additional content to the paper. 
While we made a limited number of additions, this was guided by two key 
considerations: first, the importance of maintaining a concise and accessible 
document; and second, the need to stay within the typical level of detail expected in 
an Application Paper. 
• All suggestions were carefully reviewed, and while not all could be incorporated at 
this stage, several may inform future IAIS work.
