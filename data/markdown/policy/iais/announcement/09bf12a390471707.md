2PUBLIC
Background information
• Outcome of 2014 Self-Assessment and Peer Review (SAPR): 
supervision of corporate governance not proactive enough  
• Corporate governance-related problems often root cause of 
insurer’s failures
• The response of the IAIS:
o Application Paper on the Composition and the Role of the Board 
(endorsed by the Executive Committee on 7 November)
o Application Paper on Proactive Supervision of Corporate 
Governance (covered by this public discussion session)

3PUBLIC
Overarching issues
• Nature of the Application Papers:
o Provide further advice, illustrations, recommendations or examples of 
good practice to supervisors on how supervisory material could be 
implemented
o Do not include new requirements (in addition to those set out in 
Insurance Core Principles)
• Proactive supervision of corporate governance should be 
carried out:
o Proportionately to the issues identified
o Using an approach customised to the circumstances
• Legislative changes
o May be needed when a supervisor chooses to implement suggested 
supervisory practices
o It is up to the supervisor to take the appropriate steps to do so

4PUBLIC
Issues addressed in the Application Paper
• Supervisory organisation, culture and processes
o Various challenges, related to issues such as supervisory expertise, 
supervisory ”blind spots”, willingness to act
• Gathering relevant information from different sources
o The supervisor needs to have relevant information to be able to carry 
out proactive supervision and may consider a range of approaches to 
gathering such information
• Yellow and red flags 
o Early warning system of mainly non-financial (but also financial) 
indicators of corporate governance problems
o Proposed flags structured in several categories 
• Effective communication with insurers and the wider public
o Includes sharing with insurers and the wider public the objectives and 
strategy of supervision and principles guiding the supervisory approach 
o Possible different forms of communication (verbal or written; directed 
to the entire industry or selected insurers facing the same issues)

6PUBLIC
Main comments and their resolution 
• Insurers are ultimately responsible for having a sound corporate 
governance framework
o Reflected explicitly in para. 3.
• A variety of organisational and governance structures
o Highlighted in para. 6. 
• Supervisory steps vs supervisory measures
o The paper in general refers to early supervisory steps and focuses on 
a stage of identifying and analysing corporate governance problems.
o The meaning of supervisory steps (which are different form supervisory 
measures) explained in para. 7.  
• Identification of yellow and red flags does not necessarily 
indicate broader corporate governance-related problems
o Already reflected in the wording of para. 33 and 34. Para. 33 further 
redrafted to address this point.
• Description of some yellow and red flags redrafted and new 
flags added
