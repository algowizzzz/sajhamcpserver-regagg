14PUBLIC
Background information 
• ICP 8 already revised on 2015 together with other governance-
related ICPs.
• Aims of the 2018 revision: to ensure consistency and remove
overlaps between ICP 8 and ICP 16.
• Main changes in draft revised ICP 8:
o Standard ICP 8.1 capturing the outcome-focused content of material
removed from ICP 16 (ICP 16.2, 16.3, 16.8 (2nd and 3rd bullets), 16.9
and 16.10
o Additional explanations provided in new guidance material
o Some guidance text added to cover text removed from ComFrame
guidance (applicable not only to IAIGs), based on the outcome of the
2017 published consultation on ComFrame material
o Some guidance text deleted (redundant or duplication)

15PUBLIC
Main public consultation comments
• Responses to changes in the risk profile in a timely manner
(third bullet in ICP 8.1)
o To delete 'in a timely manner' - wording kept as it provides sufficient
flexibility
o To refer to material changes – not agreed as the insurer should be able
to respond to any change. The materiality of the change is relevant for
how the insurer responds
• To remove a requirement to have a risk management policy
o In the IAIS view the insurer should have such a policy
o Policies or policy: singular used across the ICP (there should be one
policy)
• “Appropriately documented”
o To delete “appropriately” – agreed
o The requirements to document the risk management system and the
system of internal controls kept
Comments on revised standards ICP 8.1 and ICP 8.2

Background information 
• ICP 15 and 16 last adopted in 2011
• Purpose of 2017 revisions that were subject to public 
consultation November 2017 – January 2018:
o update and harmonise language with the integration of ComFrame
o add ERM-related terms to the IAIS glossary for clarity
o remove overlaps between ICP 8 and ICP 16
• Purpose of 2018 revisions:
o reflect feedback from member and stakeholder comments
o improve the clarity and consistency with other ICPs

19PUBLIC
Main public consultation comments
• ICP standards and guidance are in some cases too prescriptive 
and limit the necessary flexibility for supervisors and insurers
• Investments should not be considered in isolation in ICP 15, but 
take into account the specifics of the insurer’s liabilities and 
asset/liability management strategy (ALM)
• ICP 15 should not have overlap with other ICPs (ICP 17 in 
particular, regarding fungibility) nor should it contradict them.
• Inconsistencies in terminology used in ICP 15 and 16 should be 
resolved.
Comments on revised ICP 15 and ICP 16 and ERM-related terms

ICP 20 (PUBLIC DISCLOSURE)
Markus Grund, Chair of the Accounting and Auditing Working Group
Public Discussion Session

25PUBLIC
Background information 
• ICP 20 was adopted in 2011. 
• The 2018 revisions, subject to a Public Consultation (June to 
August), were were initiated to address ICP 20 clarity and 
consistency issues noted within the IAIS Self Assessment and 
Peer Review (SAPR) findings.
• One new substantive material is a short section to cover 
practices for non-GAAP disclosures an ICP 20 enhancement 
following discussion with IOSCO.

26PUBLIC
Main comments and their resolution
• In many jurisdictions, Supervisors do not have the authority to prescribe 
the disclosure requirements for general purpose financial statements .
o Revised draft ICP 20 to remove language in the ICP standards, where the 
Supervisor may lack legal authority.
• Some of the disclosure requirements would require the publication of 
proprietary and confidential information.
o ICP 20 includes guidance on proprietary and confidential information, which 
discusses the need for the supervisor to strike a balance between the need for 
meaningful disclosure and the protection of proprietary and confidential 
information. 
• The guidance in revised ICP 20 is overly-prescriptive and detailed. In 
fact, parts of ICP 20 go beyond information relevant to give policyholders 
and market participants insights into insurers' business activities, risks, 
performance, and financial position
o With the revisions made based on the consultation, and outside of the 
addition of one requirement on non-GAAP measures, the substance of the 
revised ICP 20 is unchanged.  The IAIS has not received feedback to date 
that ICP 20 is overly-prescriptive and detailed, and notes that the substance of 
ICP 20 is consistent with a general purpose financial statement requirements 
in many jurisdictions.

27PUBLIC
Main comments and their resolution
• ICP 20 should explicitly recognise that applicable jurisdictional 
disclosure requirements, which serve the same objective, should 
be considered as an appropriate implementation of this ICP.
o ICP 20 includes a statement in the guidance that “public general purpose 
financial reports may largely comply with jurisdictional disclosure 
standards that are reflective of this ICP.”

APPLICATION PAPER ON THE COMPOSITION AND THE
ROLE OF THE BOARD
Annick Teubner, Chair of the Governance Working Group
Public Discussion Session

30PUBLIC
Background information 
• Follow up on the outcome of the 2014 Self-Assessment and Peer 
Review: the need for more proactive supervision of governance by the 
insurance supervisors
• Scope of the paper: formal and behavioural aspects of the functioning 
of the Board
• Nature of the Application Papers:
o Provide further advice, illustrations, recommendations or examples of good
practice to supervisors on how supervisory material could be implemented
o Do not include new requirements (in addition to those set out in the ICPs)
o “Should” used as an indication of a good practice
• Public consultation (29 June – 13 August 2018)
o 15 participants
o 260 comments

31PUBLIC
Main comments and their resolution
• The principle of proportionality
o The explanation of the role of the proportionality principle added to the
Introduction
• Need for legislative changes
o If supervisors decide to implement a suggested supervisory practice, it is up
to them to take the appropriate steps, including legislative changes
• Prescriptiveness and intrusiveness of proposed supervisory practices
o Supervisors should apply suggested practices as needed and relevant.
o Proposed practices kept, but the wording clarified or the purpose explained.
• Supervision of behavioural aspects (Section 9)
o Consideration of behavioural aspects necessary for proactive supervision of
corporate governance.

ISSUES PAPER ON INCREASING DIGITALISATION IN
INSURANCE AND ITS POTENTIAL IMPACT ON CONSUMER
OUTCOMES
Myungshin Yoo, Policy Advisor, IAIS Secretariat
Public Discussion Session

38PUBLIC
Objective of the Issues Paper 
• The Issues Paper addresses digitalisation from a conduct of 
business perspective focusing on the product design and 
underwriting along with marketing, sales and distribution 
aspects of the insurance value chain (specific attention is given 
to robo advice and price comparisons websites). 
• Digitalisation can be of benefit to consumers. It could also give 
rise to risks that impact fair consumer outcomes (as promoted 
by the Conduct of Business requirements in ICP 19).

39PUBLIC
Structure of the Paper
• Section 2 considers the impacts of digitalisation on product design 
and underwriting;
• In section 3, robo advice and price comparison websites are 
considered to illustrate digitalisations impact on the marketing, sale 
and distribution of those products; 
• In section 4, the paper assesses the challenges supervisors face in 
responding to these changes.
• Section 5 contains conclusions and recommendations:
o Digital innovations can potentially improve the customer experience 
and reduce insurers’ operating cost. 
o Due attention needs to be given to achieving fair customer outcomes 
in terms of suitability of products and soundness of IT processes 
including design and use of algorithms and use of customer data.
o Supervisors need to balance risks of innovations against the benefits 
for policyholders and the insurance sector.

44PUBLIC
Development of the Application Paper
• The material on recovery and resolution is new to the ICPs and
ComFrame.
• Therefore, the IAIS will develop a set of Application Papers to
provide more background and address issues raised during the
2017 consultation.
• The 1st Application Paper is on Recovery Planning. It provides 
guidance with respect to draft (revised) supervisory material on 
recovery planning in ICP 16.13 and ComFrame 16.13.a/.b. 
Background and objectives

45PUBLIC
Development of the Application Paper
• The development of the Application Paper benefited from inputs
from both IAIS Members and Stakeholders:
o In Q1 2018, a survey was conducted amongst relevant members on
experiences and good practice with recovery planning
o On 12 September 2018, IAIS organised a stakeholder event in Basel
• Consultation period on Application Paper: 12 November 2018 –
7 January 2019.
• During 2019: finalise the Paper, taking into account public
consultation comments and ensuring alignment with ICP and
ComFrame.
• November 2019: expected adoption, together with revised ICPs
and ComFrame.
