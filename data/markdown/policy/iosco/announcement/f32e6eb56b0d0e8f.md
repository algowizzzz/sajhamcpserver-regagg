Executive Summary 
Context and background 
 
1. Collective investment schemes (CIS) have a crucial role in global 
financial markets, providing investors with pooled exposure to diverse 
assets. Accurate and reliable valuation of these schemes is central to 
investor protection, market integrity, and financial stability. 
 
2. The final report consolidates and updates the previous 2007 
Principles for the Valuation of Hedge Fund Portfolios and the 2013 
Principles for the Valuation of Collective Investment Schemes, 
reflecting recent developments in asset management, increased 
complexity of investment products, and the growing exposure to less 
liquid, illiquid and private assets. The R ecommendations in this report 
are also informed by lessons learned from periods of market stress, 
including recent episodes of market volatility. 
 
3. The overarching objectives of these Recommendations are to facilitate 
fair and consistent asset valuation across CIS, strengthen governance, 
oversight, and accountability of the valuation process, promote 
transparency and disclosure to investors and regulators, and mitigate 
potential systemic risk arising from inaccurate/inconsistent valuations. 
 
4. This report and its R ecommendations provide a globally relevant 
framework to securities regulators, fund managers, and other 
applicable stakeholders responsible for ensuring robust valuation 
practices, and to promote consistent approaches across jurisdictions. 
 
The Recommendations 
 
5. The Final Report consolidates a comprehensive set of 
Recommendations, organized around the following key areas: policies 
and governance, conflicts of interest, valuation methodologies, use of 
third party valuation service providers, consistency in valuation, pricing 
errors, timely valuation, disclosure practices and record-keeping. 
 
6. The Recommendations apply broadly to registered / authorized public 
open-ended funds except money market funds (MMF) (please refer to 
below section on scope for more detail) and cover a broad spectrum 
of the valuation ecosystem, encompassing responsible entities or fund 
managers, internal valuation functions and committees, and third-party 
valuation service providers. 
 
7. Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) are included within the scope of the 
report, with recognition of their unique features. It is recogni zed that,

5 
 
 
while most investors trade ETF shares on secondary markets, the NAV 
calculation remains crucial. Accordingly, the R ecommendations apply 
to ETF valuation processes, with proportionate consideration of 
features specific to ETFs (such as dual pricing mechanisms between 
primary market NAV and secondary market price). The objective is to 
ensure that ETFs are treated consistently with other OEFs while 
reflecting pertinent differences where applicable.  
 
8. By providing a modernized, comprehensive framework for valuations, 
the final recommendations reinforce IOSCO’s commitment to 
protecting investors, promoting market integrity, and enhancing 
financial stability.  
 
9. Accurate and robust valuation is essential not only for investor 
confidence but also for maintaining the integrity of the wider financial 
system, particularly in periods of market stress. These 
Recommendations provide regulators and market participants with a 
clear, internationally viable framework to strengthen valuation 
practices across jurisdictions.

10. IOSCO sets out Recommendations in this document that draw upon, 
but supersede, the 2013 CIS Principles and the 2007 Hedge Fund 
Principles. The revisions are intended to ensure that IOSCO ’s CIS 
valuation standards remain relevant to current market practice. In 
particular, they intend to account for recent market developments. In 
recent years there has been an increase in CIS holding less liquid and

Terminology  
For simplicity, unless otherwise stated we use the following terms for the 
purpose of this report:  
Asset – All types of assets in a fund’s portfolio. The types of assets that a fund 
holds may vary according to the fund’s investment objectives and applicable 
regulations. For example, assets include, but are not limited to, equity and fixed 
income securities, positions in derivatives, other financial instruments, private 
assets, and short positions. Assets do not include liabilities such as borrowing 
for leverage purposes. 
CIS and fund(s) – OEF(s) unless the context otherwise specifies. 
Fair value – The price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer 
a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the 
measurement date.
17 In general, fair value is defined in accordance with the 
applicable accounting standards in a jurisdiction unless the applicable 
regulations require otherwise. 
Illiquid assets – Illiquid assets including those for which there is little or no 
secondary market trading and buying and selling assets is difficult and time 
consuming (i.e. weeks or months, not days) even in normal market conditions. 
Individual transactions of illiquid assets may, therefore, be more likely to affect 
market values. 
Less liquid assets – Assets whose liquidity is contingent on market conditions, 
but which would generally be readily convertible into cash without significant 
market impact in normal market conditions. In stressed market conditions, they 
might only be readily convertible into cash at a significant discount and their 
valuations might become more difficult to assess with certainty. 
Liquid assets – Likely to be assets that are readily convertible into cash 
without significant market impact in both normal and stressed market 
conditions.18 
 
 
17 Definition of “fair value” according to International Financial Reporting Standards 
(IFRS) 13 and Accounting Standards Codification (ASC) 820 under the US 
Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP). 
18 The three definitions on liquid, less liquid and illiquid assets were introduced by 
IOSCO’s Revised Recommendations for Liquidity Risk Management for Collective

Open Ended Funds (OEFs)  – Funds within the intended scope of the 
Recommendations, defined  as registered / authori zed public open -ended 
funds, including ETFs but excluding MMFs.  
Other Funds – Funds outside the intended scope of the Recommendations for 
which the Recommendations may serve as potential Good Practices.  
Private assets – Assets that generally are not publicly traded. Examples of 
private assets include private equity, private debt, real estate, infrastructure etc. 
The term private finance is also used. As set out in IOSCO’s ‘Thematic Analysis: 
Emerging Risks in Private Finance – Final Report’ (2023)’, the term “private 
finance” is broad, and definitions can vary across jurisdictions.19 
Responsible entity – The entity / entities responsible for the overall operation 
of an  OEF and in particular its compliance with the legal / regulatory 
framework in its respective jurisdiction (e.g., the fund manager or the fund 
board). The Recommendations are focused on the entity/entities responsible 
for the overall operation of the OEF, and their implementation may vary from 
jurisdiction to jurisdiction, depending on local conditions and circumstances. 
Review – The term here is used in the general sense of the word to review or 
assess (e.g. periodic review by the responsible entity of the valuation policies 
/ procedures including the valuation methodologies), not as understood as a 
technical term under applicable accounting/auditing/attestation standards or 
terminology in the applicable jurisdiction and/or any professional engagement 
or missions.  
Securities Regulators  – The authorities which are empowered to authori se, 
supervise and/or enforce relevant rules and legislation relating to the 
operation of CIS or their managers in their respective jurisdictions. 
Third Party Valuation Service Providers  – External and independent service 
providers which provide valuation services to the responsible entity. For 
substantive discussion, see Recommendation 7 (on page 37) on use of Third 
Party Valuation Service Providers.

Chapter 2: Overview of key 
updates 
21. This chapter sets out the key updates in this Final Report. In addition 
to merging the Principles into a unified set of Recommendations, these 
are:  
 
a) Oversight arrangement. Recommendation 1 sets out that the 
responsible entity should establish an appropriate oversight 
arrangement and valuation function as part of the fund’s valuation 
governance framework.  
 
b) Governance under stressed market conditions. Recommendation 
1 sets out additional guidance on how the responsible entity could 
ensure that governance arrangements are in place to deal with 
stressed market conditions. 
 
c) Management of conflicts of interest. Recommendation 2 sets out 
a framework for managing conflicts of interest including additional 
examples of conflicts and mitigations.  
 
d) Fair value. Recommendation 3 sets out that the responsible entity 
should ensure that assets are valued at fair value in line with 
applicable regulations, accounting standards, and rules and articles 
of incorporation of the CIS.  
 
e) Back testing. Recommendation 3 provides additional guidance on 
back testing and calibration (see Methodology below on page 25), 
which could be important tools to allow the responsible entity to 
review the appropriateness and accuracy of valuation 
methodologies and processes.  
 
f) Use of Third-Party Valuation Service Providers. Recommendation 
7 enhances requirements on how the use of third  party valuation 
service providers  should be properly documented in the fund’s 
valuation policies and procedures (e.g. the circumstances under 
which third party valuation service providers are used and for which 
asset types, the assessment of the independence and qualification 
of the third party valuation service provider, the process by which 
their inputs/reports are provided to the responsible ent ity and 
included in the valuation process,  etc.). 
 
g) Stale or inaccurate  valuations. Recommendation 9 enhances 
timely valuation requirements by setting out that the responsible

14 
 
 
entity should ensure that the policies and procedures take into 
account how to address stale or inaccurate valuations. 
 
h) Record keeping. There is a n ew Recommendation 13 on record 
keeping by the responsible entity to demonstrate compliance with 
its valuation obligations. Records often provide the primary means 
to document whether assets have been valued in a manner 
consistent with applicable law, valuation policies and procedures, 
and disclosures. They also provide evidence to third parties, 
including auditors, to enable them to perform their duties related to 
audits of financial statements. They also facilitate effective 
regulatory oversight.

71. Back testing and calibration can be important tools to allow firms to 
review and test the appropriateness and accuracy of fair value

identify certain market trends, as well as potential poor performance 
or conflicts of interest by the entity or third parties performing 
valuations. Back-testing involves comparing the reali zed value of an 
asset upon exit to the last fair value valuation to assess and understand 
potential biases or other broader trends in the valuation

selected.

dates so as to ensure that subsequent model -based valuations are 
consistent with initial fair value assumptions and prevailing market 
conditions. For example, key assumptions may include the 
discount/premium applied to multiple or asset- specific risk premiums 
in a discounted cash flow analysis.

Q23: Are there other aspects of timely valuation that this Recommendation should 
address? 
Detailed summary of the feedback: 
Respondents generally agreed with the content of the Recommendation. Two 
respondents advocated for defining stale by asset class and one respondent stated 
that activating liquidity management tools (LMTs) would be more appropriate than 
proceeding to a re -valuation of assets where there are heightened redemptions. 
Some respondents also noted that stale valuation refers to valuations which have 
not been updated despite new information, and that some valuations in some asset 
classes may be stable over a long t ime. For the latter, the importance of accurate 
valuation is the key consideration, rather than the length of time since the last 
valuation. 
IOSCO’s response:   
IOSCO has edited the explanatory text of the Recommendation to distinguish 
further the respective treatment of less liquid and illiquid assets and to provide 
more illustrative examples. Lastly, IOSCO considered that the activation of LMTs is 
not a response to inappropriate valuation and that valuation at fair value should be 
ensured before LMTs, apart from suspensions, are deployed.

Q25: Are there other aspects of pricing errors that should be addressed in this 
Recommendation? 
Detailed summary of the feedback: 
There was near -unanimous agreement among respondents that no additional 
aspects of pricing errors needed to be addressed beyond those already covered 
in the Recommendation. Suggestions for amendments included linking materiality 
more explicitly to investor outcomes and c onsidering patterns of repeated or 
systemic pricing errors.  
IOSCO’s response:   
IOSCO considers that the existing framework appropriately addresses the 
identification, assessment and remediation of pricing errors, and that the suggested 
additions would risk increasing complexity without delivering commensurate 
regulatory or investor -protection benefits. IOSCO therefore does not intend to 
amend the Recommendation in this respect. 
Q26: Do you agree with the creation of a new Recommendation on record keeping? 
Detailed summary of the feedback: 
Most respondents supported creating a specific recommendation on 
recordkeeping for valuations, emphasizing its importance given increasing valuation 
complexity and frequency. Some questioned the need for a separate 
recommendation given existing regulatory requirements. 
There were further individual suggestions by two respondents on being more 
explicit/further emphasizing why record-keeping is important for valuations and to 
be more explicit about when record-keeping is important for valuations. 
IOSCO’s response:

IOSCO notes the feedback received regarding whether a new, separate and
