# 2022 Global Seminar

With the resonant theme of insurance sector resilience, the IAIS Global Seminar welcomed over 350 participants from 70 countries to Dubrovnik, Croatia, on 16-17 June 2022.

Together, panellists and participants engaged and exchanged on the steps needed to strengthen insurance sector resilience following the unprecedented challenges of the past two and a half years.

## Panel discussions

### Town Hall

##### Updates from IAIS committees with moderated Q&A

The Global Seminar started with an engaging and interactive session where IAIS Committee Chairs, Vice Chairs and Champions provided updates on key IAIS projects and delivery against the **[IAIS Roadmap](https://www.iaisweb.org/about-the-iais/strategic-plan-and-roadmap/)**.

***It is good to benefit from the extra inspiration and energy that come from interacting directly with colleagues, reminding ourselves of our core goals and objectives as we head into the final lap for several projects agreed in 2019 and strive for agility in responding to emerging risks over the horizon.***

Following the progress updates, the floor was opened to a questions from the audience. Stakeholders probed further on the opportunities of sectoral and international coordination in area of diversity, equity and inclusion, FinTech and cyber; next steps and timelines in the ICS project; and the effective and efficient global approach to addressing climate risk and the insurance protection gap.

### Inclusive insurance

##### How inclusive insurance can support sustainable development

The Covid-19 pandemic and current bleak economic outlook have exacerbated social inequality across both developed and emerging market economies. This panel discussed how inclusive insurance can address inequality and promote sustainable development.

***Supervisors need to think creatively and act differently to foster financial inclusion, understand who the underserved are and how appropriate regulation can support financial inclusion.***

Panellists contemplated obstacles to and opportunities for innovative solutions to increase inclusive insurance, including technological innovation, public-private risk-sharing initiatives to address protection gaps and possible changing product offerings to meet customer needs. Panellists also stressed the importance of financial education.

### Operational and cyber risk

##### Addressing accelerating operational and cyber risks in the insurance sector

At a time of increasing cyber risks, this panel focussed on the IAIS’ work on operational risks, cyber resilience and cyber underwriting risk.

The panellists discussed several complex issues, including IT concentration risk, service providers management, single point of failure, the current state of the cyber insurance market, data availability and model maturity.

***Panellists agreed that talent shortage is one of the main issues affecting the insurance sector’s exposure to cyber risk.***

This panel also considered the work the IAIS is undertaking to gather data on cyber risks as part of this year’s Global Insurance Market Report special topic edition.

### Holistic Framework

##### Finalising implementation of a comprehensive approach to assessing and responding to global market risks and trends

During this session, panellists discussed IAIS members’ approaches to and experience with the implementation of the Holistic Framework’s key elements of risk monitoring and supervisory policy measures for macroprudential purposes.

***Industry and supervisors shared views on how the Holistic Framework is a significant step forward in how the lAlS is monitoring, assessing and responding to the potential build-up of systemic risk in the insurance sector.***

Strengths of the Holistic Framework consist of taking a sector-wide and individual insurer view, seeking out systemic risks from common activities and exposures essential to keep abreast of emerging financial stability risks. Panellists also highlighted the benefits of bringing the global and jurisdictional perspective together.

### Climate scenario analysis

##### Next steps in the journey to develop effective climate risk assessment

Given the systemic nature of climate risk and the critical stewardship role of insurers in the transition to net-zero, understanding and addressing the risks from climate change are a key priority for the IAIS.

***Both the insurance sector and insurance supervisors are on a journey when it comes to climate risk scenario analysis and should see themselves as together on that urgent journey.***

Panellists considered insurance supervisors’ practices on climate risk scenario analysis, focussing on the steps the IAIS is taking to support capacity building and international collaboration. The panel also investigated the importance of data for effective analysis and how this informs the climate data being gathered as part of the IAIS’ annual Global Monitoring Exercise.

### Insurance Capital Standard

##### Countdown to the finalisation of the global solvency standard

As the IAIS reaches the mid-point of the ICS five-year monitoring period, IAIS members from four regions shared their perspectives on the development of the global standard. This session also served as the background session for the recently launched **[public consultation](https://www.iaisweb.org/consultations/)** on the draft criteria that will be used to assess whether the Aggregation Method provides comparable outcomes ICS.

***Participation in the ICS monitoring period is critical to help shape the final standard.***

Panellists reflected on the development of the global standard, lessons learnt during the monitoring period and progress on the comparability assessment. Audience discussion centred on how stakeholder feedback will help to shape the finalisation of the ICS as a prescribed capital requirement.
