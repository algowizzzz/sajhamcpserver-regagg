We’ve updated our rules on information sharing during UK equity initial public offerings (IPOs) to reduce market risk and frictions in the UK IPO process for issuers and market participants.

## What we are changing

We’ve removed certain requirements from our rules.

Firms no longer need to wait 7 days between publishing an approved registration document/prospectus and connected research. This will reduce the length of the IPO process by 7 days for most issuers and lower market risk.

Firms publishing connected IPO research no longer need to share the same information with a range of unconnected analysts as they do with their own research analysts. This will improve the flow of information to analysts and reduce compliance costs and burdens for issuers.

## Who this is for

This Policy Statement affects:

* prospective issuers
* retail and institutional investors in shares admitted to a UK regulated market
* investment advisers, brokers and other intermediaries
* independent research providers (IRPs)
* investment banks and other firms involved in the IPO process
* law firms and sponsors involved in the IPO process

## Next steps

Firms and issuers can still choose to engage with unconnected analysts during the IPO process. This is no longer required by our rules. Any such arrangements should be agreed on a commercial basis.

We will continue to consider the feedback to our discussion questions on the remaining aspects of the 2018 reforms. We welcome further input on this, and the role of research in equity markets more widely.

## Background

Our 2018 rules were designed to encourage independent research during the UK equity IPO process ([PS17/23](/publications/policy-statements/reforming-availability-information-uk-equity-ipo-process "PS17/23: Reforming the availability of information in the UK equity IPO process")). But the rules did not always work as intended. Instead, they added costs and market risk for issuers without clear benefits.

We consulted on changes in [CP26/14 (PDF)](/publication/consultation/cp26-14.pdf "cp26-14.pdf").

These changes support our objective to enhance market integrity by removing unnecessary risk and friction from the listing process. They also advance our secondary objective of supporting the competitiveness and growth of UK markets – helping companies access capital and giving investors access to a wider range of investment opportunities.

We also included discussion questions on the remaining aspects of the 2018 IPO information flows rules to help explore where there are more opportunities to reform these rules.

### [Investment research on IPOs](#expandable1914206)

In UK IPOs, pre-deal investment research is commonly provided by banks to their institutional investor clients. This may be published either by syndicate banks providing deal-related services to the issuer (connected research), or by non‑syndicate firms and independent providers that distribute research to their own institutional client base (unconnected research).

We first introduced the rules on unconnected analysts in July 2018. This followed concerns raised in discussion paper [DP16/3 (PDF)](/publication/discussion/dp16-3.pdf "DP16/3 Availability of information in the UK Equity IPO process") about the lack of availability of information during the UK equity IPO process and potential policy options for improving the quality of this information.  Following industry engagement, the suggested changes were made into final rules in [PS17/23 (PDF)](/publications/policy-statements/reforming-availability-information-uk-equity-ipo-process "PS17/23: Reforming the availability of information in the UK equity IPO process").
