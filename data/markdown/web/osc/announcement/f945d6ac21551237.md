# How to register a firm

Both firms and individuals who advise on or trade in securities or commodity futures, contracts or options, or who manage investment funds in Ontario, must register under the Ontario Securities Act.

Once we have received a firm’s registration application, we will notify each firm when to submit [individual applications](/en/industry/registration-and-compliance/getting-registered/how-register-individual).

## National Registration Database enrolment

A firm applying for registration must first enroll with the [**National Registration Database (NRD)**](https://www.nrd.ca/) to obtain its NRD number. Enrolling with NRD enables the firm to submit individual registration filings and select firm filings electronically.

For more details, refer to:

* [National Instrument 31-102 *National Registration Database*](/en/securities-law/instruments-rules-policies/3/31-102)
* [OSC Rule 31-509 *National Registration Database* (Commodity Futures Act)](/en/securities-law/instruments-rules-policies/3/31-509)

Canada’s provincial and territorial securities regulators recognized the New Self-Regulatory Organization of Canada (New SRO), effective January 1, 2023. New SRO subsequently changed its name to the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) on June 1, 2023. CIRO consolidates the functions of the Investment Industry Regulatory Organization of Canada (IIROC) and the Mutual Fund Dealers Association of Canada (MFDA). CIRO regulates mutual fund dealers, investment dealers, and acts as the regulation services provider for marketplaces that have retained it as such, including monitoring trading on those marketplaces for compliance with CIRO rules and securities legislation.

## Firm registration under Ontario’s Securities Act

If the firm is applying for registration as a dealer, adviser or investment fund manager under [Ontario’s Securities Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90s05_e.htm) only in Ontario, complete [Form 33-109F6 *Firm Registration*](/sites/default/files/2023-10/ni_20230913_33-109F6_unofficial-consolidation.pdf "Form 33-109F6 Firm Registration") and submit it to the OSC and/or CIRO, as applicable.

On April 1, 2025, the OSC will delegate the registration function for investment dealers, mutual fund dealers and futures commission merchants to CIRO. CIRO will be publishing guidance on how to submit a Form 33-109F6 with them.

#### Registering in more than one province or territory

Except for restricted dealers, firms must use the passport and interface system if they want to register in more than one province or territory:

* if the firm’s principal regulator is in Ontario, submit Form 33-109F6 and all supporting documents only to the OSC or CIRO, as applicable
* if the firm’s principal regulator is not in Ontario, submit Form 33-109F6 and all supporting documents to the principal regulator and submit a copy of the form to the OSC or CIRO, as applicable, without the supporting documents

Once registered in any jurisdiction, firms can apply to register in additional provinces or territories. For more information, please refer to [changing categories or jurisdictions](/en/industry/registration-and-compliance/changes-registration/changing-registration-categories-or-jurisdictions).

A firm should also follow this process when applying simultaneously for registration under Ontario’s [Securities Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90s05_e.htm) and [Commodity Futures Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90c20_e.htm).

#### Restricted dealer

A firm applying to register as a restricted dealer **cannot** use the passport and interface system and must submit a separate Form 33-109F6 to each regulator. For more information on restricted dealers, please refer to part 7 of [National Instrument 31-103 *Registration Requirements, Exemptions and Ongoing Registrant Obligations*](/en/securities-law/instruments-rules-policies/3/31-103) (NI 31-103).

#### Investment dealers and mutual fund dealers

On April 1, 2025, firm registration for investment dealers and mutual fund dealers in Ontario will be delegated to CIRO. After this date, a firm seeking registration as an investment dealer or mutual fund dealer must submit a Form 33-109F6 and all supporting documents to the [**Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO)**](https://www.osc.ca/en/industry/market-regulation/self-regulatory-organizations-sro/canadian-investment-regulatory-organization-ciro). CIRO will be publishing guidance on how to submit a Form 33-109F6 to them.

#### Dual registered firm

A firm may be both an investment dealer and mutual fund dealer (dual registered firm) if they are a member of the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO). If you have any questions or are interested in applying for registration as a dual registered firm, please contact your principal regulator and CIRO.

#### Registering in more than one firm category

Beginning April 1, 2025, a firm seeking registration as an investment dealer or mutual fund dealer combined with another firm registration category that has not been delegated to CIRO, must submit a Form 33-109F6 and all supporting documents to both the OSC and CIRO.

## Firm registration under Ontario’s Commodity Futures Act

If the firm is applying for registration as a dealer or adviser under [Ontario’s Commodity Futures Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90c20_e.htm) only in Ontario, complete [Form 33-506F6 *Firm Registration*](/sites/default/files/2020-11/form_33-506f6.pdf) and submit it to the OSC through the filing portal. Firms are not required to answer questions 1.4(a), 2.6 and 6.2 on the form. You may also wish to refer to [OSC Rule 33-506 *Registration Information*](/en/securities-law/instruments-rules-policies/3/33-506).

#### Registering in more than one province or territory

If the firm wants to register in more than one province or territory, it must submit a separate application to each province’s or territory’s regulator. Once registered in any jurisdiction, firms can apply to register in additional provinces or territories. For more information, please refer to [changing categories or jurisdictions](/en/industry/registration-and-compliance/changes-registration/changing-registration-categories-or-jurisdictions).

#### Futures commission merchant

Beginning April 1, 2025, firm registration for futures commission merchants will be delegated to CIRO. After this date, a firm seeking registration as a futures commission merchant must submit a Form 33-109F6 and all supporting documents to the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO). CIRO will be publishing guidance on how to submit a Form 33-109F6 with them.

## Guide to completing and filing a firm application

#### Form 33-109F6

When a firm has determined which category of registration they wish to apply under, they must complete and file [Form 33-109F6 *Firm Registration*](/sites/default/files/2020-11/form_33-506f6.pdf) in accordance with [**Ontario’s Securities Act**](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90s05_e.htm), if applying for registration in Ontario.

A firm must submit the applicable supporting documents identified in Form 33-109F6 along with its completed form.

Depending on the firm registration categories the firm is applying for, they must submit the Form 33-109F6 and all supporting documents through the [**OSC’s electronic filing portal**](/en/securities-law/filing-documents-online) and/or to CIRO, as applicable. CIRO will be publishing guidance on how to submit a Form 33-109F6 with them.

#### Form 33-506F6

Form 33-506F6 for firms seeking registration under [Ontario’s Commodity Futures Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90c20_e.htm) is completed in a similar manner to Form 33-109F6. Please refer to the [Guide to Completing and Filing a Firm Registration Application](/sites/default/files/2020-11/form-registration-guide.pdf) to assist with the completion of Form 33-506F6.

## Fees for registering

The firm application fee for registering under [Ontario’s Securities Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90s05_e.htm) is $1,300, regardless of the number of categories you apply for. An additional fee of $1,300 is required for firm applications under [Ontario’s Commodity Futures Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90c20_e.htm). Refer to [fees](/en/industry/registration-and-compliance/registration-forms-and-documents/fees) for more information.

Once your application is submitted, you will be contacted by OSC or CIRO registration staff, as applicable, who will provide you with instructions on how to pay the firm application fee which, generally, is paid through the firm’s electronic fund transfer account on NRD.

Where a firm is exempt from the requirement to pay fees through NRD, the firm will remit the fees via cheque or wire transfer.

If the firm is applying for registration in other jurisdictions, [additional regulatory fees](https://www.nrd.ca/) are required for those jurisdictions. These are indicated on, and can be paid through, NRD.

## Passport and interface registration system

The process of registration under this system is set out in [National Policy 11-204 *Process for Registration in Multiple Jurisdictions* and Multilateral Instrument 11-102 *Passport System*](/en/securities-law/instruments-rules-policies/1/11-204). This system allows the firm   to submit the application with its principal regulator, which reviews the application on behalf of all the regulators in the other jurisdictions (the non-principal regulators) in which the firm is applying for registration. The firm communicates only with the principal regulator.

If the principal regulator is in Ontario, the application is processed under the **passport system.** The OSC or CIRO, as applicable, conducts a review of the application and its decision is effective in the other jurisdictions.

If the principal regulator is not in Ontario, the application is processed under the **interface system**. The principal regulator reviews the application and the OSC or CIRO, as applicable, decides whether to opt in or opt out of the principal regulator’s decision. The OSC or CIRO can perform its own review.

## Application review and approval

Approval is based on the information provided in the registration application form and supporting documents, through background checks of individuals, public information and from other information we may request.

Firms which have submitted an application to the OSC will be added to NRD in a pending state. We will then request the firm to submit their registration application fees, generally through their electronic funds transfer account with NRD.

We will review the application and will communicate with the firm by providing comments and/or requesting additional information when necessary. A filing that is “complete” means a filing of acceptable quality that contains the necessary analysis and addresses relevant issues. An incomplete or deficient application will delay the review process.

When considering your application, we conduct a pre-registration meeting with the key principals of the firm, including the proposed chief compliance officer, the ultimate designated person, or both to:

* understand the firm’s business objectives
* provide guidance
* determine whether the firm has the appropriate individuals and compliance structure in place to meet the ongoing regulatory requirements for registered firms.

If it appears that the firm is not suitable for registration or that the proposed registration would be objectionable based on public interest, the OSC may refuse the registration or grant the registration subject to [terms and conditions](/en/industry/registration-and-compliance/changes-registration/registration-terms-and-conditions). In these cases, OSC staff will send the applicant firm a letter providing written notice of its recommendation regarding registration and brief reasons for it. This is referred to as a **Letter of Brief Reasons**. Section 31 of [Ontario’s Securities Act](http://www.e-laws.gov.on.ca/html/statutes/english/elaws_statutes_90s05_e.htm) and Section 23(3) of [Ontario’s Commodity Futures Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c20#BK91) then provides the applicant with [an opportunity to be heard before the Director](/en/industry/registration-and-compliance/opportunity-be-heard-and-directors-decisions) prior to a decision being made concerning staff’s recommendation.

CIRO will be publishing guidance on their review and approval process.

## Timeline to review and approve a registration application

We are committed to reviewing and processing your applications promptly and professionally, while fulfilling our regulatory responsibilities to address any compliance or policy issues that may arise.

For more information on the OSC’s service standard timing to review firm registration materials, see the [OSC Service Commitment](/about-us/accountability/osc-service-commitment). Firms are notified by e-mail when their registration is approved.

Prescribed conditions for new business applications are that:

* you are a non-SRO applicant
* all questions are answered with sufficient detail
* all regulatory obligations are met
* there are no concerns with your fitness for registration
* you respond to our request for information in a timely manner

Although we strive to meet or exceed our service commitments, certain key factors may affect the timeliness of our decision:

* the completeness and accuracy of your filings
* the complexity of the issues they raise
* timeliness of your response to our request for information
* concerns with your fitness for registration

CIRO intends to publish guidance on their service commitments.

In cases where there appear to be issues with an application that could bear on the applicant’s suitability for registration, such as past misconduct or untrue or misleading information given in the application itself, the file may be referred by the Registration Team to the Registrant Conduct Team for further investigation, requiring a longer review time. Each phase in the registration process when applications transition from the Registration Team to the Registrant Conduct Team are illustrated in this [process chart](/sites/default/files/2021-05/How-to-register-process-chart.pdf).

If you have questions about the registration process, please [contact us](/en/about-us/contact-us "contact us").

If you have questions about registering under your particular circumstances, you should seek professional advice. As a regulatory agency, the OSC cannot provide legal or other professional advice.
