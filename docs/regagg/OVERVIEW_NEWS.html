Copyright & ToS posture — deliberate, not incidental

  • We store the headline, the publisher’s own feed summary, the link, and the timestamp.
  • Enforced in code — the news pipeline has no article-fetch path at all; an automated test fails the build if one is ever introduced.
  • Attribution on every record — each story links back to the publisher, who keeps the reader and the full text.
  • We never fetch, store, or republish article bodies — including from paywalled sources.

Where licensed feeds plug in

Bloomberg, Reuters/LSEG, and the ratings agencies (S&P, Moody’s, Fitch) retired their public feeds — their content is licensed. The lane was built to receive them: a licensed feed is a config file, not a code change, and full-text rights would simply widen what we may store.

For first-hand ratings actions — the single highest-value addition for a credit desk — an agency subscription is the procurement step; the platform side is already done.
Today the credit-events bucket is populated by second-hand coverage (BNN, Globe, WSJ). It works, but a direct ratings feed would make it authoritative.
